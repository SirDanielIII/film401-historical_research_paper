In 1976, Vlahos founded Ultimatte to develop electronic compositing equipment. His later patent addressed uneven screens, shadows, reflected blue light and partial transparency. Electronic circuits compared colour signals and combined foreground and background without the repeated printing stages of a film laboratory.

This extended his work into television production as well as film. He received an Emmy in 1978, and he and his son Paul received an Academy Award for the Ultimatte process in 1995.

Today’s digital keyers still have to separate subjects carefully and control unwanted backing colour. In 2024, Paul Debevec and Corridor Crew even revisited sodium-vapour matting with digital cameras.

Vlahos’s career connects several generations of filmmaking technology. His contribution was to make separately photographed elements easier to combine convincingly, giving production crews more control over the finished image.
