Before Petro Vlahos ever worked on a movie, he was designing aircraft components and working with radar. Not exactly the career you'd expect from someone who would become so important to special effects, but the world works in strange ways.

He was born in New Mexico in 1916 and grew up in California. After studying engineering at Berkeley, he worked at Douglas Aircraft during World War II, and later at Bell Labs.

While working there, he met an MGM sound director who introduced him to the Motion Picture Research Council. This was where engineers worked on technical problems that Hollywood studios were struggling to solve.

And the timing was important. Television was becoming increasingly popular, Hollywood's studio system was changing, and filmmakers were looking for ways to make movies more visually impressive.

Vlahos entered the industry at a time when engineering was becoming increasingly important to filmmaking. And one of the biggest challenges was making separately filmed images or shots, look convincing together.
