If you look at the scene from Mary Poppins, where Dick Van Dyke is dancing with animated penguins, they seem to share the same physical space. But they were filmed separately, long before digital effects software.

So, how did Disney combine them so convincingly? The answer involves a yellow screen, a modified camera, and engineer Petro Vlahos.  His story shows how much film history actually happens behind the camera, rather than in front of it.
