Vlahos was born in Raton, New Mexico, in 1916. His family moved to San Pedro, California, when he was eight. After earning an engineering degree at Berkeley in 1941, he worked at Douglas Aircraft during the war and later as a radar engineer at Bell Laboratories. An MGM connection led him to the Motion Picture Research Council.

Hollywood was changing. Television competed for audiences, while antitrust decisions and HUAC investigations unsettled the studio system. Those events are historical context, not proven causes of his inventions. Studios invested in widescreen films and expensive spectacles, and shared research helped crews adapt. Vlahos brought engineering experience to an industry with increasingly demanding pictures to make.
