Vlahos was born in Raton, New Mexico, in 1916. His family moved to San Pedro, California, when he was eight. He graduated from Berkeley in 1941, worked at Douglas Aircraft during the Second World War, and later became a radar engineer at Bell Laboratories.

At Bell Labs, an MGM sound director introduced him to the Motion Picture Research Council, which coordinated technical work across studios. That connection brought him into filmmaking. After the council dissolved in 1960, he worked outside film before returning as chief scientist at the Motion Picture Research Center in 1968. The centre was operated by the Association of Motion Picture and Television Producers.

Hollywood was under pressure. Television drew audiences into their homes, and studios invested in widescreen spectacles to bring them back. The Paramount antitrust decision was changing the studio business, while HUAC investigations and blacklisting threatened film workers’ careers. Within this unsettled industry, shared laboratories gave engineers like Vlahos a place to develop tools for increasingly demanding productions.
