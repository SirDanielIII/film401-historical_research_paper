Vlahos had also been developing sodium-vapour photography, filing that patent in 1958. Here the separation happened inside the camera.

Sodium lamps illuminated the backdrop with narrowband yellow light near 589 nanometres. The actors received separate, ordinary lighting. A specially coated prism separated the sodium band from the rest of the scene light. Colour film recorded the performance, while black-and-white film recorded the matte at the same instant.

Those matched records helped preserve fine detail when Disney combined live actors with animation in Mary Poppins. Vlahos developed the prism system, and Ub Iwerks helped adapt the camera for Disney’s productions. Wadsworth E. Pohl shares their Academy technical award, presented in 1965.

It was a collaborative achievement. The specialized camera, lamps and optics also limited the system’s flexibility, helping explain why Vlahos continued working on other approaches.
