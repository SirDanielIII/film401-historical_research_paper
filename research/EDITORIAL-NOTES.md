# Editorial decisions

The assignment is a historical study of a figure: origins, career path, social/political/industrial context, contributions and legacy. It is not reduced to a green-screen tutorial. The research has no imposed word ceiling; the five narration chapters alone are kept near five minutes.

The expanded dossier is the factual foundation. The refined script supplies the spoken progression, adapted into five recordings: introduction, biography/context, earlier mattes plus colour difference, sodium/Mary Poppins, Ultimatte/legacy. Research paragraphs and narration are separately written. The outline for the formal 800-word paper remains in the full dossier; the website does not assume instructor approval as a substitute submission.

The main argument is precise separation and greater flexibility, rather than invention of the coloured screen. Biography appears near the start. Political context is expanded with clearly marked supplementary government and archive sources. Wartime employment is documented; a direct derivation of compositing patents from radar, defence funding or personal politics is not claimed.

Filing and grant dates remain distinct. Sodium's 1958 filing is earlier than the 1959 colour-difference filing, even though the narrative groups blue-screen work first. Ben-Hur illustrates format constraints; individual chariot shots are not assigned a process without evidence.

Credit is shared. The Academy record anchors the names Vlahos, Wadsworth E. Pohl and Ub Iwerks; the limits of assigning Pohl an individual component remain explicit. Foster's inventor-interview emphasis is compared with Disney's institutional emphasis. British sodium work and contradictory prism-count accounts remain visible. Awards years and ceremony years are distinguished.

The modern camera-sensor material is an analogy about recorded colour information. Vlahos is not credited with Bayer, CCD or CMOS inventions. Corridor Crew's video is identified as a modern reconstruction and does not settle historical attribution or surviving-prism counts.

The personal website reference supplies the themes, palette, Berlin Sans typography, rounded media, restrained hovers and spacing. The guide article reference supports system-font long prose. The exhibit remains modular and standalone because the reference ZIP is not the active main application.

Seventeen local media assets replace the former diagram-only presentation. Each has a centralized source and rights record. Image downloading and resizing did not manufacture any archival evidence. Patents were obtained from their source records; camera/interview illustrations were extracted from the cited handbook; source photographs were visually inspected before captions were assigned. Uncertain public redistribution rights remain visible in the image register. Disabled assets are excluded from the standalone build, not merely hidden in the UI.

The RGB interaction now uses actual photographic colour data. Its key is a deliberately limited modern teaching model; the selectable original illustration demonstrates additional glass/hair cases. The sodium demonstration retains an optical explanation with simultaneous colour and matte records. Neither is claimed to recreate an entire historical laboratory procedure.
